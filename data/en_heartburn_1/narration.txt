If your chest burns after meals and something sour keeps creeping up your throat, here's what's really behind it.

A ring of muscle called the lower esophageal sphincter keeps stomach acid where it belongs. When it relaxes too much, acid pushes back up into your esophagus - and certain foods make that muscle relax right on cue.

Coffee is one of the most common triggers doctors hear about. Caffeine relaxes that sphincter muscle and ramps up acid production, especially when you drink it on an empty stomach.

Try switching to decaf, or have your coffee after a meal instead of before - that alone takes a lot of the sting out.

Spicy food is right behind it. In one survey, nearly half of people with reflux said spicy meals made their symptoms worse - capsaicin irritates the esophagus lining as it goes down, and slows digestion, which gives stomach acid more time to work its way back up.

You don't have to cut spice completely. Swapping hot sauce for milder herbs like basil or oregano still gives you flavor without the burn.

Tomato sauce is the sneaky one - pizza, pasta, ketchup. Tomatoes are naturally acidic, and in patient surveys about one in ten reflux sufferers point to tomato dishes specifically as a trigger.

Look for a low-acid tomato sauce, or just stir a pinch of baking soda into your regular sauce - it helps neutralize some of that acidity.

None of these need to disappear completely - just watch the timing and portions, and pay attention to what your body's actually telling you after each meal.
