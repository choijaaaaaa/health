Legs or stomach suddenly cramping up after a heavy sweat session? If any of these three sound familiar, it's probably not just tight muscles.

Heat cramps come from a mix of salt loss, fluid loss, and muscle fatigue, not just muscle tightness on its own. When you sweat heavily, you lose sodium along with water, and that throws off the signals your nerves send to your muscles. Three things make it worse: being a heavy salty sweater, drinking only plain water, and pushing through tired muscles.

First, some people are simply heavier, saltier sweaters than others. If you notice white, crusty marks on your skin or clothes after a workout, that's a sign you're losing more sodium than most. Add extra salt to your sports drink, or grab a salty snack during long workouts, instead of relying on plain water alone.

Second, drinking large amounts of plain water during exercise dilutes the sodium you have left, which can trigger cramps even faster. Reach for a sports drink with electrolytes, or pair water with a salty snack or a piece of fruit like a banana.

Third, ignoring aching, fatigued muscles and continuing to push through your workout raises your cramp risk, since tired muscles lose fine motor control. When a muscle starts to cramp, stop, gently stretch and massage it, then rest in a cool spot and sip an electrolyte drink while you recover.

Salt, fluids, and muscle fatigue - manage those three together and heat cramps become far less likely. If cramps keep coming back or last a long time, it's worth checking in with a doctor.
