Ever feel dizzy, lightheaded, or like you're about to faint after standing in the heat too long? That's probably heat syncope, not just "feeling overheated."

When your body gets hot, it pumps extra blood toward your skin to cool down, and that pulls blood flow away from your brain. Add in fluid loss from sweating, and your blood pressure can drop fast enough to make you feel faint or actually pass out for a few seconds. Three habits make this far more likely: standing up too fast, staying in direct sun without breaks, and pushing through the warning signs.

First, standing up quickly after sitting or lying down in the heat. Your blood vessels are already widened from the heat, so they can't react fast enough to the sudden change, and blood flow to your brain drops for a moment. Pause for a few seconds before you stand, and once you're up, stay put for a moment before you start walking.

Second, standing in direct sun for long stretches, like at a graduation, a concert, or a parade. Staying in one position lets blood pool in your legs instead of circulating back to your heart, so less oxygen reaches your brain. Move to shade every 20 to 30 minutes, and if you have to keep standing, pump your calf muscles by shifting your weight or rocking on your feet.

Third, ignoring the warning signs and pushing through, like lightheadedness, tunnel vision, or a cold sweat. Those are your body telling you blood flow to your brain is already dropping, and continuing to push through raises your risk of actually going down. When you notice them, sit or lie down right away and raise your legs above heart level. Move somewhere cooler, loosen tight clothing, and sip water or a sports drink slowly.

Standing up slowly, taking shade breaks, and listening to the warning signs - manage those three and heat syncope becomes far less likely. If you actually lose consciousness or the dizziness doesn't go away after resting, it's worth getting checked out.
