Turns out, a few everyday foods could be the reason your hands and feet feel numb, cold, or swollen by the end of the day.

When blood vessels narrow and circulation slows down, that's usually what's driving it. Too much sodium makes your body hold onto extra fluid, and too much saturated fat builds up along the vessel walls. Deli meat, fried food, and sugary soda are three of the biggest everyday culprits.

A two-ounce serving of deli turkey packs around 570 milligrams of sodium - that's more than a third of the American Heart Association's ideal daily limit, in just one sandwich.

Freshly roasted chicken breast at the deli counter is a much lower-sodium swap than cured meats. If you do stick with packaged deli meat, check the label for a low-sodium or no-salt-added version.

Fried food is next. Regularly eating it is linked to higher rates of heart disease and stroke - the trans fat in the oil raises LDL cholesterol and damages artery walls over time.

Air-frying or baking at home uses a fraction of the oil, and grilled or roasted options are an easy swap when you're eating out.

Sugary soda is the third one. Just one sugary drink a day is linked to a nearly 20 percent higher risk of heart disease.

Swap it for sparkling water with a splash of real fruit juice, or unsweetened iced tea instead.

Small swaps in what you reach for add up to real relief for your circulation over time.
