Senile purpura affects up to 30 percent of adults over 75, those dark bruises that show up on your arms with no memory of bumping anything. There are three main reasons behind it. First, years of sun exposure break down collagen in the skin, especially on the hands and forearms. That makes the skin thinner and leaves blood vessels with less support. Second, blood thinners like aspirin make it easier for blood to leak out once a vessel tears, so the bruise spreads much larger than it should. Third, dry skin loses its elasticity, so it absorbs even less impact than before. Here is the reassuring part: these bruises usually fade on their own within one to three weeks and leave no scar. Still, a few habits help. Cover your hands and forearms with long sleeves or sunscreen before you head outside. Keep track of the medications you are taking, and check with your doctor if bruising gets frequent or severe. And moisturize daily, since a 12-week study found that daily retinol cream actually thickened skin and boosted collagen. Bruising easily is not something to worry about, it just means your skin needs a little more care.
