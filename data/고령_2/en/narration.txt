Sarcopenia — if climbing stairs or opening a jar feels harder than before, it's usually not just age. It's often these three habits.

A low-protein breakfast, cardio-only workouts, and swapping meat for soft carbs are the three biggest culprits behind muscle loss.

A typical breakfast of cereal and toast covers only a fraction of the 25 grams of protein your muscles need in one sitting. Add two eggs, Greek yogurt, or a glass of milk instead of loading up at dinner.

Walking protects your heart but barely challenges your muscles, which is why daily walkers can still lose strength. Add resistance training with bands or dumbbells twice a week to improve grip strength and muscle mass together.

When chewing gets harder, skipping steak is natural, but replacing it with rice or pasta instead of another protein source deepens the gap. Try ground meat, scrambled eggs, silken tofu, or boneless white fish instead.

Sarcopenia isn't just about age. Fix breakfast, add resistance training, and choose protein you can chew — those three habits alone can meaningfully slow muscle loss.
