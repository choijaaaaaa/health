About 1 in 2 women over 50 will break a bone from bone loss they never even felt coming - here's what's speeding it up.

When your blood calcium dips even a little, your body pulls calcium straight out of your bones to make up the difference. Do that over and over, and your bones slowly hollow out until a small fall or a hard cough is enough to break one.

Three everyday drinks make that happen faster: soda, coffee, and alcohol.

Soda is the biggest one. The phosphoric acid in cola pulls calcium out of your body, and one long-running study found women who drank cola regularly had close to 4% lower bone density in the hip - even the diet and caffeine-free versions.

Swap soda for sparkling water, or a calcium-fortified drink, and if you still want one, keep it to a single can a day so it doesn't chip away at your calcium as much.

Coffee's a factor too. Caffeine makes your body flush out calcium through urine, and the risk really shows up in women who drink 4 or more cups a day without enough calcium elsewhere in their diet.

Cap it around 2 to 3 cups a day, and add a splash of milk for extra calcium.

Alcohol is the third one. Heavy drinking - more than 2 drinks a day - has been linked to a 38% higher risk of any fracture, and a hip fracture risk up to 68% higher.

Heavy drinking can also interfere with your liver's ability to activate vitamin D, so build in a few alcohol-free days each week, and make sunlight or vitamin D-rich food a daily habit - that's what actually helps your body use the calcium you're already getting.

Watch what you're drinking, pair it with enough calcium, and you can protect your bones before a fracture is what tips you off.
