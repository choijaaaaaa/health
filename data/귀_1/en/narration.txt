Ringing that shows up after time at the range, a few vapes, or just getting older? Loud shots, nicotine, and a common vitamin gap can each trigger tinnitus in very different ways.

A single gunshot can hit 150 to 165 decibels, past the one-shot limit NIOSH sets at 140. That much noise can damage the inner ear instantly, and ringing is often the first sign.

Nicotine in vapes narrows the tiny blood vessels feeding the inner ear. Less oxygen reaching those cells is linked to new or worse tinnitus, especially with frequent e-cigarette use.

Low vitamin B12 can wear down the nerves in the inner ear. One study found nearly half of people with noise-related hearing loss and tinnitus were low on B12, especially those eating little meat or dairy.

Wear earplugs around gunfire or loud equipment. If vaping is a daily habit, cutting back - even by half - may ease the strain on inner-ear blood flow, and a doctor can help if quitting feels hard. And if your diet is light on meat and dairy, ask about a B12 test or fortified supplement.
