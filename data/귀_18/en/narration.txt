Your AirPods hearing test just came back looking fine? Here's what to check before you trust that result completely.

Apple's Hearing Aid feature got FDA clearance as the first over-the-counter hearing aid software, and a clinical study found self-fit users did about as well as those fitted by a professional. Still, three things are worth knowing: who it's for, what the test can't catch, and all-day wear.

First, it's cleared only for adults with mild-to-moderate hearing loss. Severe loss, and any sudden or one-sided loss, are explicitly excluded — the software isn't built for those cases.

Second, there's no professional fitting behind it. You calibrate it yourself through an in-app tone test, so adjustments a real audiologist would normally catch can get missed.

Finally, it's not built for all-day wear the way a real hearing aid is. Battery runs around six hours, and leaving earbuds in your ear canal for hours raises the risk of irritation.

If a hearing test comes back showing sudden or one-sided loss, don't just turn up the amplification and move on. See an ENT doctor first — catching the actual cause early is what protects your hearing.
