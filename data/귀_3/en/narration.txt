Most people think ear infections only happen to kids. They don't — and it usually comes down to one tiny tube. Your Eustachian tube connects your middle ear to your throat and balances pressure. When it stays blocked, fluid pools behind your eardrum and bacteria move in.

First, riding out a cold or sinus infection instead of treating it. The longer inflammation lingers, the longer your tube stays shut, and fluid keeps collecting behind your eardrum.

Second, skipping swallowing or yawning during takeoff and landing. Pressure shifts fast, and without your tube opening to match it, pressure builds and pulls fluid out of the lining.

Finally, regular exposure to cigarette smoke, yours or someone else's. It damages the tiny cilia that sweep fluid out of your Eustachian tube, so drainage slows down.

Treat colds and sinus infections early. Chew gum or swallow during takeoff and landing. Keep your distance from smoke, direct or secondhand — your Eustachian tube handles the rest.
