Sudden hearing loss, could loud headphones or unchecked blood pressure be putting your ears at risk? This is a medical emergency. Sudden sensorineural hearing loss needs treatment within 72 hours for the best chance of recovery. Here's what raises your risk, and the one mistake that makes it worse.

First, loud noise. Frequent high-volume headphone use and loud venues like concerts damage the hair cells inside the cochlea, raising your risk of a sudden hearing drop.

Second, cardiovascular risk factors. High blood pressure, diabetes, and high cholesterol are all linked to sudden hearing loss, since they restrict blood flow to the inner ear.

Beyond those risk factors, one habit makes the outcome far worse: waiting it out. If one ear suddenly feels muffled, or you notice ringing or dizziness, brushing it off costs you time you can't get back.

Keep headphone volume moderate, and stay on top of blood pressure and blood sugar checkups. Most importantly, see a doctor the same day if you suspect sudden hearing loss. Earlier treatment means better odds of recovery, so don't wait.
