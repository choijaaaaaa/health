Torn meniscus - if your knee buckles or gives way going down stairs, are you brushing it off?

Between your thighbone and shinbone sits a C-shaped cushion of cartilage called the meniscus, and a tear happens when that cartilage rips. That buckling feeling on stairs comes from the joint losing stability, and you may also notice catching, clicking, or a knee that won't fully straighten - about 850,000 meniscus procedures happen in the US every year.

There are two very different causes. In young, active people, it's usually a forceful twist with the foot planted on the ground, common in soccer, basketball, and football. In older adults, the cartilage simply wears down over time, so a tear can happen with no injury at all, sometimes from something as small as stepping on uneven ground.

Ignoring a torn meniscus speeds up arthritis. Once the cartilage can't cushion the joint anymore, bone grinds against bone, and that accelerates cartilage loss and knee osteoarthritis.

First, rest the knee and ice it for 15 to 20 minutes every 3 to 4 hours for a few days. Skip deep squats and twisting movements until the swelling calms down.

Second, build quad strength. Sit with your leg straight, tighten your thigh muscle, and hold for 5 seconds - stronger quads take pressure off the joint itself.

See a doctor right away if your knee gets stuck bent or straight and you can't move it, if you can't bear weight, or if swelling gets worse fast.
