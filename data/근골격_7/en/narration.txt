Most people blame frozen shoulder on overuse or bad posture — research says that's usually not it. Frozen shoulder is actually the opposite of an overuse injury, driven by three quieter factors instead.

First, keeping the shoulder still too long after an injury or surgery. Skip the stretching, and the joint capsule never lengthens back out — it stiffens in that shortened position.

Second, poor blood sugar control. Chronic high glucose creates advanced glycation end products that bond to collagen in the capsule, making it stiffer and more prone to scarring. One meta-analysis found diabetics have nearly 3.7 times higher odds of frozen shoulder.

Finally, untreated thyroid problems. Low or imbalanced thyroid hormone disrupts collagen remodeling around the joint, and studies link thyroid disease to roughly 2.7 times higher risk.

Move your shoulder gently within a pain-free range after an injury, manage blood sugar, and get thyroid symptoms checked. If motion is severely limited, see a doctor — early treatment helps a lot.
