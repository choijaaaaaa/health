Doctors have nicknames for this one: "texting thumb" and "mommy thumb." Both point to the same thing — two tendons running through a narrow tunnel at the base of your thumb getting irritated, usually from one of three everyday habits.

First, gripping your phone with one hand and scrolling with just your thumb. That repeated spreading and bending motion inflames the tendon sheath until the tunnel gets even tighter.

Second, a repeated gripping motion from racquet sports, weightlifting, or yard work. Doing the same grip over and over strains those same tendons, even if your phone habits are fine.

Third, lifting and holding a baby with your thumb propping up the weight. Do that dozens of times a day, and it adds up the same way.

Hold your phone with both hands and switch your grip often. Warm up before gripping activities and switch hand positions, and support a baby's weight with your whole palm and forearm instead of just your thumb. See a doctor if the pain lasts more than two weeks or the swelling gets worse.
