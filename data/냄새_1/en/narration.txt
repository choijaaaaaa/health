Notice people take a small step back mid-conversation, even hours after your shower? Something you ate might be leaking out through your sweat.

Body odor forms when food compounds are absorbed and released through sweat and breath - and some foods produce far more odor molecules than others.

Garlic and onion contain sulfur compounds like allicin, which can keep releasing through sweat and breath for over 24 hours after you eat them.

Red meat like beef takes longer to digest, and that slower breakdown can produce more compounds that make sweat smell stronger.

Beer and wine break down into acetic acid, released through sweat - that's often what gives it a sour, vinegar-like edge.

Cook garlic and onion instead of eating them raw, swap red meat for white fish or chicken breast sometimes, and drink water alongside alcohol. Fresh vegetables and herbs can help too.
