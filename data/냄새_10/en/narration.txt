Groin odor, if it gets noticeably worse in summer no matter how often you shower, pay attention — that's usually not a hygiene problem. Skin folds trap heat and moisture, and that warm, damp environment is exactly what odor-causing bacteria and fungi thrive in.

Sweat itself barely smells. It's bacteria breaking down sweat and oil on the skin that creates the musty odor, and skin folds block airflow just enough to keep bacteria multiplying nonstop.

Three habits make it worse: staying in tight, sweaty underwear too long, not drying the area fully after showering, and scratching persistent itch instead of addressing it.

Tight synthetic underwear traps heat and moisture against the skin, speeding up bacterial growth. Switch to breathable cotton, and change out of sweaty underwear as soon as you can after a workout.

Leftover moisture after a shower keeps the area damp for hours, and that lets odor build. Pat the area completely dry with a towel — if you also deal with athlete's foot, dry your feet last, since the same fungus causes both and spreads easily between them.

Scratching breaks the skin and lets bacteria in, which can make the odor worse and lead to inflammation.

If the odor comes with redness, flaking, or a sharply-bordered rash, that's not just sweat — it could be jock itch, a fungal infection also called tinea cruris. See a dermatologist for antifungal treatment rather than scratching through it.

Breathable underwear and thorough drying alone make a noticeable difference in summer groin odor.
