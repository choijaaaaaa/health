Body odor — if you notice a sour, sharp smell the morning after whiskey or wine, your body is still working through what you drank.

There are three main reasons. How alcohol breaks down, what you drank, and your own body chemistry.

First, the breakdown process. Your liver converts alcohol into acetaldehyde before turning it into acetic acid. Acetaldehyde itself carries a sharp, sour smell, and part of it escapes through your breath and your skin before your body finishes processing it.

Second, the drink itself. Whiskey, wine, and other dark spirits carry far more congeners than clear spirits like vodka — bourbon alone holds dozens of times more than vodka does. Those extra compounds take longer to break down, so the smell lingers.

Third, your body chemistry. Many people, especially of East Asian background, carry a weaker version of the enzyme that clears acetaldehyde, so it stays in the system longer even at the same amount consumed.

Sweating it out won't speed things up. Your liver processes roughly one drink an hour, no faster. Staying hydrated and giving it time is what actually works.
