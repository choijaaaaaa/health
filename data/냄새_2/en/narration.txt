Scalp odor that won't go away even after washing? Pay attention to these three habits.

Your scalp has more oil glands than your face. Within four to twelve hours after washing, that oil starts to oxidize, and by twelve to twenty-four hours bacteria and yeast break it down into odor compounds. These three habits speed that process up.

First is relying on dry shampoo instead of a real wash. It masks oil temporarily, but the residue builds up and traps oxidizing sebum right against the scalp.

Second is rinsing too fast. Leftover shampoo and conditioner residue mixes with sebum and breaks down into a stronger odor.

Third is using a dirty hairbrush. An unwashed brush can hold thousands of bacteria colonies per square inch, and every stroke sends that oil and bacteria straight back onto clean hair.

Wash dry shampoo out with a real shampoo every few uses instead of skipping a wash entirely. Spend a full minute rinsing shampoo right at the scalp, not just through the length of your hair. And wash your hairbrush weekly with warm, soapy water. These three changes make scalp odor noticeably better.
