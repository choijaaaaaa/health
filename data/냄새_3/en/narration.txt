Bad breath, if mints and mouthwash never last long, stop guessing what's causing it. Most of what's left behind isn't coming from where a quick fix can actually reach.

Gum disease, dry mouth from medication, and postnasal drip are three common causes that mints and mouthwash can't fix.

First, gum disease. Once plaque builds up below the gumline, bacteria trapped there break down tissue and release sulfur compounds, and dentists call that smell "perio breath." Surface brushing never touches what's happening under the gums.

Second, dry mouth. Over a thousand common medications, including antihistamines and antidepressants, cut down how much saliva you produce. Saliva normally washes bacteria away, so less of it lets odor-causing bacteria build up faster.

And third, postnasal drip. Allergies or sinus congestion send mucus down the back of your throat instead of out through your nose, and that trapped mucus mixes with bacteria and starts to smell on its own.

Floss daily and get a professional cleaning for gum disease, stay hydrated and try sugar-free gum for dry mouth, and use a saline rinse for postnasal drip. All three get noticeably better once you treat the actual source.
