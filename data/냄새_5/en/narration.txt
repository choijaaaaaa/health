Morning breath, if brushing before bed never even crossed your mind, pay attention — that small habit matters more than most people think. Saliva production drops sharply while you sleep, and that's what lets odor-causing bacteria take over.

Anaerobic bacteria on the back of your tongue break down proteins into sulfur compounds overnight. That's the smell you notice the moment you wake up.

Brushing and scraping your tongue right before bed, not just in the morning, can cut how strong morning breath feels by roughly half — it removes the bacteria's food source before hours of low saliva let them multiply.

Mouth breathing makes it worse. Sleeping with your mouth open, from congestion or snoring, dries out your mouth all night. A saline rinse before bed and bedroom humidity between 40 and 60 percent help you breathe through your nose instead; if congestion keeps coming back, get it checked out.

Dehydration adds to it too — not enough water, or alcohol before bed, means even less saliva to begin with. Spread your water intake through the evening instead of all at once, and try a cup of green tea earlier in the day: its polyphenols neutralize sulfur compounds and curb the bacteria that make them.
