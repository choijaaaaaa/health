If your hands and feet feel like ice even indoors, it's not just the weather - a few common American foods can be making your circulation worse.

Coffee and energy drinks are one culprit. A 2013 study found caffeine blunts your fingers' natural rewarming response to cold - try capping coffee at one or two cups a day.

Salt is another. Most Americans get too much sodium before they even touch a salt shaker, mostly from processed food, and it can damage blood vessel walls - check labels and reach for low-sodium options.

Fried food adds up too. One high-fat meal can temporarily cut blood flow through your arteries - swap fried chicken or fries for grilled or baked versions more often.

None of these need to disappear completely. Cutting back, and adding ginger or a pinch of cayenne pepper, linked to better blood flow, can help warm up cold hands and feet.
