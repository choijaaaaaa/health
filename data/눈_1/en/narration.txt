Headlights turning into starbursts on your drive home at night, and your eyes feeling dry and gritty by the time you park? A dry, uneven tear film is a common cause of glare at night - and it's not just "getting older."

Soda spikes blood sugar, linked to cataracts and macular degeneration - cut back on it, and add lutein-rich kale, which has 25 times more lutein than carrots.

Fried food triples the risk of late-stage macular degeneration in one 18-year study - salmon's omega-3s help slow that down.

Scrolling in the dark cuts your blink rate under 8, raising dry eye risk - try the 20-20-20 rule instead: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds.

Macular damage is hard to reverse, so start with what you eat - and give your eyes a break from the screen once it's dark out to keep the dryness from piling on top.
