Glaucoma is often called the silent thief of sight, and if you have not had a baseline eye exam since turning 40, it is worth checking now.

Fluid inside your eye cannot drain properly, so pressure builds and presses on the optic nerve. Unlike macular degeneration, which blurs central vision first, glaucoma creeps in from the edges, so most people do not notice until real damage is done.

Here is what surprises people: many with glaucoma test in the normal eye pressure range, called normal-tension glaucoma. A pressure check alone can miss it, so ask for a visual field exam too.

Risk climbs after 40, with a family history, or if you are Black or Hispanic, since glaucoma is six to eight times more common in Black Americans. That is why the American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends a baseline eye exam at 40 for everyone.

Head-down yoga poses like downward dog can spike eye pressure within a minute, while regular aerobic exercise like walking or swimming tends to lower it.

Sudden severe eye pain with headache and nausea, or rainbow-colored halos around lights, is an emergency. Vision loss can happen within a day or two, so get to an eye doctor right away.

Glaucoma cannot undo damage already done, but catching it early protects the vision you still have.
