Ever caught a bright red patch on your eye in the mirror and had no idea it was even there? That's usually a subconjunctival hemorrhage - a broken blood vessel under the clear surface of your eye. It looks dramatic, but most of the time there's no pain and no change in your vision.

It happens when sudden pressure - what doctors call the Valsalva effect - pushes back into the fragile vessels around your eyes. A hard cough, a sneeze, vomiting, heavy lifting, or straining on the toilet can all trigger it.

Rubbing itchy or tired eyes, or pressing too hard when you put in contact lenses, adds that same kind of pressure directly.

If you're on aspirin or a blood thinner, your blood doesn't clot the way it normally would, so even a small break bleeds more and takes longer to clear - and it tends to happen more often if you also have high blood pressure.

A cold compress for the first ten to fifteen minutes helps with any swelling, then switching to a warm compress after a few days speeds up reabsorption. For discomfort, reach for acetaminophen instead of aspirin, and never stop a prescribed blood thinner on your own - check with your doctor first.

Most cases clear up within one to three weeks without any treatment. If you notice pain, blurred vision, or this keeps happening, it's worth getting checked by an eye doctor.
