Cataracts are the leading cause of vision impairment worldwide, and by 2020 an estimated thirty million Americans were living with one. Beyond blurry vision, the fuller symptom picture includes trouble seeing at night, sensitivity to glare, needing brighter light to read, seeing halos around lights, colors that look faded or yellowish, and needing new glasses more often than usual. Age is by far the most common cause, since lens proteins break down over decades, but that breakdown speeds up dramatically when a few other factors are in the mix.

Diabetes and high blood pressure are two of the biggest accelerators. Both conditions speed up how fast lens proteins denature, so people managing either one often develop cataracts earlier and watch them progress faster. Keeping blood sugar and blood pressure within your target range is one of the most effective things you can do.

UV exposure is the next major factor. Ultraviolet energy generates free radicals that attack the proteins and fats in your lens, and over years those damaged proteins clump together and scatter light instead of letting it pass through cleanly. Sunglasses labeled UV400 block those damaging wavelengths, and a wide-brimmed hat adds coverage your sunglasses can't reach on their own.

Smoking, heavy alcohol use, and long-term steroid medication round out the list, and all three add oxidative stress directly to the lens. Here's something worth knowing: research on popular lutein and zeaxanthin supplements hasn't shown they lower cataract risk on their own, even though they're often marketed that way. What the evidence does support is eating antioxidant-rich whole foods, like leafy greens and citrus, rather than counting on a pill to do the work.

Managing your blood sugar and blood pressure, wearing UV-blocking sunglasses, and cutting back on smoking and alcohol all add up to real protection for your lens. But if your vision clouds suddenly or you start seeing double in one eye, don't wait it out — see an eye doctor right away.
