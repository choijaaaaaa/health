Photokeratitis, if your eyes start burning and feel sandy hours after a sunny afternoon outside, check this now — your cornea may have picked up a UV sunburn you never felt happen.

Photokeratitis is basically a sunburn on the clear front surface of your eye. Because the damage builds up gradually like a skin burn, you don't feel it in the moment — studies show symptoms can take anywhere from thirty minutes to twelve hours to show up, and eye pain often wakes people up in the middle of the night after a day outside. Skipping UV-blocking sunglasses on bright days, not realizing tanning beds and welding arcs give off the same damaging rays, and pushing through the pain instead of treating it properly are the three habits eye doctors see most.

First is spending hours at the beach, on the water, or on snow without wraparound UV-blocking sunglasses. Sand, water, and snow all reflect a large share of UV rays back up into your eyes, so the exposure adds up faster than it feels like it should. Grab sunglasses labeled "100% UV protection" or "UV400" before you head out, not after your eyes start to sting.

Second is not realizing that tanning beds and welding arcs give off the same UV wavelengths as the sun, just concentrated into a much shorter burst. A short, unprotected tanning session or a quick glance at a welding arc triggers the same corneal sunburn as a full afternoon at the beach. Protective goggles rated for UV are not optional anywhere near either one.

Third is rubbing your eyes and pushing through the pain instead of treating it. Photokeratitis usually heals within twenty-four to seventy-two hours once your eyes get a break from light, but rubbing raises the risk of scratching the already-damaged surface. Stay in a dim room, use preservative-free artificial tears to ease the grit and dryness, and skip contact lenses until the pain and light sensitivity are fully gone.

Wraparound UV sunglasses outdoors, real UV protection anywhere tanning lamps or welding arcs are running, and giving your eyes a dark, tear-supported break when it flares up — these three habits keep a painful afternoon from turning into repeat damage.
