Think you can catch pink eye just by looking at someone? That's a myth — but it spreads faster than most people realize, through direct contact with discharge from an infected eye. Bacteria or viruses reaching the conjunctiva — the membrane covering your eye — cause the redness, itching, and discharge.

First, touching your eyes with unwashed hands keeps the infection circulating — discharge on your fingers goes right back in.

Second, sleeping in contact lenses raises your risk. A closed eyelid traps bacteria against the cornea overnight, and rinsing lenses in tap water instead of solution adds more risk.

Finally, sharing towels or pillowcases with someone infected makes transmission far more likely — the virus survives on fabric long enough to pass along.

Wash your hands before touching your eyes, remove contacts before bed and clean them with solution, and keep towels and pillowcases to yourself until symptoms clear. See a doctor if swelling is severe or your vision changes — this is general info, not a diagnosis.
