Ever notice specks or cobwebs drifting across your vision? Most of the time that's harmless - but if the number suddenly jumps, or part of your view goes dark, that's a different story.

Eye floaters happen when clumps in the vitreous, the clear gel filling your eye, cast shadows on your retina. Aging is the top cause - the vitreous turns watery and pulls away from the retina over time, something most people go through by 65. There's no drop or pill that removes them; your brain typically adapts.

High myopia and diabetes raise the stakes, since a thinner retina or fragile blood vessels are more likely to tear or bleed. One small study also found an antioxidant nutrient blend left about 70% of users with smaller, less bothersome floaters.

The signs to take seriously: a sudden flood of new floaters, flashes of light, or a curtain-like shadow over part of your vision. Those can signal a retinal tear - see an eye doctor right away.

Floaters on their own are common. Just remember those warning signs.
