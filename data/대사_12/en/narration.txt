Triglycerides — nearly one in three American adults has a level of 150 or higher, and most don't even know it, because high triglycerides rarely cause any symptoms on their own. Three everyday habits are usually behind it.

Triglycerides are a type of fat your body stores for extra energy. Your liver makes them from leftover glucose or alcohol and releases them into your bloodstream. Unlike cholesterol, which responds mostly to saturated fat, triglycerides react much more to sugar, alcohol, and how much you move. Refined carbs, alcohol, and inactivity are the three biggest drivers.

First, refined carbs — think soda, white bread, and white rice. When you take in more glucose than your body needs right away, your liver converts the extra into fatty acids and packages them as triglycerides. Drinks high in fructose speed up this process even more.

Swap soda for water or unsweetened tea, and trade white rice or bread for whole grains like oats. Cutting back on refined carbs alone can bring your numbers down noticeably.

Second is alcohol. As your liver breaks down alcohol, it ramps up fatty acid production at the same time, and much of that ends up circulating as triglycerides. Drinking on an empty stomach makes this effect even stronger.

Cut back on how often and how much you drink. Skipping the empty-stomach drinking habit first and building in a few alcohol-free days each week both help.

Third is inactivity. Your muscles rely on an enzyme called lipoprotein lipase to pull triglycerides out of your blood and burn them for energy. The longer you sit, the less active that enzyme becomes, so triglycerides stay in your bloodstream longer.

Aim for about 30 minutes of brisk walking or similar activity most days. Moving your muscles reactivates that enzyme and helps clear triglycerides faster.

Cutting refined carbs, drinking less, and moving more — these three habits alone can bring your triglyceride level down noticeably. If your level climbs above 500, the risk of acute pancreatitis rises sharply, so don't ignore a result that high. Talk to your doctor.
