Vitamin D deficiency — if you've been wondering whether it's just you, even in the middle of summer, save this before you forget.

According to NHANES data, roughly one in four US adults have vitamin D levels too low for bone and overall health, and the rate climbs even higher during fall and winter, among women, and among people who spend most of their time indoors.

Here's the surprising part. Population studies show it's not really sunscreen driving this. Staying in the shade and covering up with long sleeves are linked to lower vitamin D levels more strongly than sunscreen use itself. The bigger driver is simply spending most of the day indoors — desk jobs, commuting by car, air-conditioned offices.

Vitamin D isn't just about bones, it's tied to metabolism too. A UK Biobank study of more than 335,000 people, published in Frontiers in Nutrition in July 2026, found that insulin resistance and vitamin D deficiency together raised cardiovascular risk by 26% more than either factor alone. Researchers now call this combination a high-risk phenotype worth screening for together.

There are four habits that help. Fortified foods, fatty fish, egg yolks, and short daily sun breaks.

First, fortified foods. Milk, orange juice, and cereal in the US are commonly fortified with vitamin D — check the label and make them part of your routine.

Second, fatty fish. Salmon, mackerel, and tuna are some of the richest natural sources of vitamin D. Aim for at least two servings a week.

Third, egg yolks. Don't stick to egg whites only — the yolk is where the vitamin D is.

Finally, short sun breaks. Stepping outside for just 15 minutes with your arms or legs exposed makes a real difference — skip the peak midday sun if you can.

Fortified foods, fatty fish, egg yolks, and short daily sun breaks — these four habits reliably raise vitamin D levels. If your last checkup didn't include a 25-hydroxyvitamin D blood test, ask for one at your next visit. And if you already have insulin resistance or a metabolic condition, don't start high-dose supplements on your own — talk to your doctor first.
