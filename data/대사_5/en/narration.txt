Insulin resistance affects more than 2 in 5 US adults, and most don't even know it. It happens when your cells stop responding well to insulin, so blood sugar creeps up over time. Three habits make it worse.

First, visceral fat. Fat packed deep around your organs releases fatty acids and inflammatory chemicals that block insulin from moving glucose into your cells.

Second, added sugar. A 7-week trial found that sugary drinks trigger fat production in the liver even in moderate doses, and that liver fat buildup drives insulin resistance right where it starts.

Third, chronic stress. Elevated cortisol promotes visceral fat storage, and more visceral fat leads to more cortisol, creating a cycle that keeps blunting insulin sensitivity.

Losing just 5 to 7 percent of body weight can cut diabetes risk in half. Swapping sugary drinks for water or whole fruit eases the load on your liver, and short stress breaks like a walk or a few minutes of breathing exercises help break the cortisol cycle.
