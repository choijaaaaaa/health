Weight loss stalled? Up to 85% of dieters hit this wall at some point. It's not a failure — here's what's really going on.

First, you might be eating more than you think. A classic NEJM study found dieters who reported eating 1,028 calories a day actually ate 2,081, a 47% gap between reported and actual intake.

Second, your metabolism adapts as you lose weight. Mayo Clinic notes that losing weight costs some muscle too, and that slows your resting metabolism even at the same calorie intake.

Third, your hunger hormones shift against you. A landmark NEJM study tracked hormone levels a full year after weight loss and found leptin and ghrelin still hadn't returned to baseline, keeping hunger elevated.

Track your food honestly for a week or two, add strength training and enough protein, and consider a short diet break at maintenance calories — plateaus are your body catching up, not the end.
