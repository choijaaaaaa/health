Bumps on your scalp that look like acne and actually hurt when you touch them? Check these three habits first.

Scalp folliculitis happens when bacteria or yeast overgrow inside your hair follicles, and hot tub exposure, tight ponytails, and heavy styling products are three everyday triggers that make it worse.

First, hot tubs and pools with off chlorine levels let Pseudomonas bacteria thrive in a biofilm on the tub surface, and an itchy, inflamed rash typically shows up just a few days after exposure.

Second, tight ponytails, braids, or hair ties pull constantly on the same follicles, and that ongoing tension causes small, painful pustules right at the pull points, even without any infection involved.

Third, heavy waxes or pomades that sit directly on your scalp trap oil and residue inside the follicle opening, and that clogged environment is exactly what bacteria need to flare up.

Rinse off and shower with soap right after a hot tub or pool, loosen your ponytail or vary your hairstyle so the same spots aren't under constant tension, and keep heavy styling products on your mid-lengths and ends instead of your scalp. Most cases clear up within two to six weeks with these changes, and a doctor visit is worth it if the pain is severe or it doesn't improve.
