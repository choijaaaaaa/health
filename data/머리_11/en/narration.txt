If you're constantly losing focus at your desk, forgetting what you walked into a room for, or rereading the same email three times, brain fog is probably why — and it eats into how much you actually get done.

Brain fog isn't a diagnosis on its own. It's a cluster of symptoms: trouble concentrating, losing your train of thought, and blanking on the right word, often all at once.

The most common trigger doctors point to is blood sugar swings. Your brain runs almost entirely on glucose, so when your blood sugar drops too low between meals, it doesn't get the steady fuel it needs, and that's when the fog rolls in. Sleep debt, chronic stress, and hormonal shifts during pregnancy or menopause stack right on top of that.

Three fixes make the biggest difference: steady meals, protected sleep, and real breaks.

First, stop skipping meals. A protein-and-fiber breakfast keeps your blood sugar level instead of spiking and crashing by mid-morning.

Second, protect seven-plus hours of sleep instead of trading it for one more episode. Sleep debt hits focus before it hits anything else.

And finally, build in a real screen-free break every couple of hours. Even five minutes away from your screen resets your focus better than another cup of coffee does.

Brain fog isn't a personality flaw or a productivity failure. Steady your blood sugar, protect your sleep, and take real breaks — the fog clears.
