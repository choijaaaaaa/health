If your hair's turning gray way earlier than your friends', it's not always genetics - three things can speed it up.

Hair gets its color from melanocytes, pigment cells that need vitamin B12, zinc, and copper to keep working. Once one of those runs short, gray strands show up faster.

First, low B12, common on a plant-based diet - one study found over half of people with premature gray hair had low B12, and since it mainly comes from meat, eggs, and dairy, vegans run a higher risk.

Second, overdoing zinc supplements. The NIH caps daily zinc at 40 milligrams, but around 60 milligrams for ten weeks can block copper absorption, and copper is what activates the enzyme that makes melanin.

Third, chronic stress. A Harvard study found stress floods hair follicles with norepinephrine, burning through the stem cells that produce pigment - once that's gone, gray doesn't come back.

If you're plant-based, add a B12 supplement or fortified foods. If you take zinc, check the label so you're not stacking a multivitamin, lozenges, and fortified snacks at once, and pair food-based zinc with copper-rich cashews or leafy greens. And build in real stress relief, like sleep and exercise, before it catches up with your hair.
