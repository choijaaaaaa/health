Fine dust reaching your gut — if that's news to you, save this now. Fine dust isn't just a breathing problem. Three things are behind it: swallowed particles, a weakened gut lining, and an imbalance in gut bacteria.

First, the mucus that traps particles in your nose and airways gets swept to the back of your throat and swallowed, several times a day. That means fine dust doesn't just stay in your lungs — it travels straight into your stomach and intestines.

Second, research shows swallowed particles can loosen the tight junctions between the cells lining your gut. Once that barrier weakens, bacteria and waste products can leak through, a pattern often called "leaky gut."

Finally, fine dust exposure has been linked to shifts in gut bacteria: fewer of the beneficial strains that produce short-chain fatty acids, and more of the strains tied to inflammation. Some studies have even connected heavy pollution exposure to a higher risk of inflammatory bowel disease.

On high pollution days, wear a mask to cut down what you swallow, and load up on fiber-rich vegetables to support your gut lining. Probiotics can also help rebuild that bacterial balance.
