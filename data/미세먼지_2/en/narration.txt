Poor air quality outside doesn't mean you're safe once you're indoors — the EPA's wildfire smoke guidance actually flips a lot of the usual advice. Three habits make indoor air worse on bad air days: cracking the windows, cooking on the stove, and running the vacuum.

First, skip cracking the windows. Opening them during a smoke event or high pollution alert lets outdoor particles straight into your home. Switch your HVAC fan to recirculate mode instead, and swap in a MERV-13 filter if your system can handle it.

Second, hold off on frying, broiling, and candles. Cooking indoors pushes particle levels far higher than what's happening outside, and it can take over an hour to clear. Cook after the air quality improves, or run your range hood on high the whole time.

Third, skip the vacuum until levels drop — it stirs settled dust right back into the air. Run a properly sized HEPA air purifier instead, and pick one that produces little to no ozone.

Keeping windows shut, holding off on cooking, and running a real air purifier make bad air days far more manageable.
