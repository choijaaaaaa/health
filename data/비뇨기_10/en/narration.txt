Urine color - about 80 to 90 percent of bladder cancer cases start with red urine that doesn't hurt at all. Here's what each urine color is actually telling you.

Urine color comes down to two things: a pigment called urochrome and how much water is in your system. The more diluted your urine is, the lighter it looks - the more concentrated, the darker.

There are three color patterns worth knowing. The yellow spectrum from pale to dark, pink or red urine, and dark brown or tea-colored urine.

First, pale straw yellow means you're well hydrated. Dark yellow or amber means your body needs more fluids - that's the most common reason urine looks darker than usual.

Second, pink or red urine usually points to a urinary tract infection, kidney stones, or an enlarged prostate. Painless red urine deserves extra attention though - it's the most common first sign of bladder cancer, showing up in roughly 80 to 90 percent of cases before any other symptom appears.

Third, dark brown or tea-colored urine can mean a liver or bile duct problem. It can also show up after an extremely intense workout, when muscle breakdown called rhabdomyolysis releases myoglobin into your bloodstream - that combination with severe muscle pain needs emergency care.

A little extra darkness usually just means you need more water. Painless red urine is the one to take seriously and get checked, and tea-colored urine along with severe muscle pain needs urgent care too.
