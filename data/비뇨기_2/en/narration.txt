If your face or feet keep swelling, or you're noticing foam in the toilet, your kidneys could already be under strain — and three everyday foods might be a big part of it.

Eating too much sodium makes your body hold onto water and pushes blood pressure up, which is what causes that swelling. Keep that pressure up for years, and it can damage the kidneys' filtering units, letting protein that should stay in your blood leak into your urine instead. The sneaky sources: deli meat, bread, and canned soup.

Two ounces of deli turkey or ham, about one sandwich's worth, carries over 500 milligrams of sodium from the curing salt alone — nearly a quarter of a day's limit.

Bread barely tastes salty, but the CDC ranks it the top sodium source in the American diet, just from how much people eat. Two sandwich slices can add up to 400 milligrams.

Canned soup swings even wider: one cup can hold 100 to over 900 milligrams depending on the brand.

You don't need to quit any of these — just check the label. A soup marked "low sodium" has 140 milligrams or less per serving, and picking it over the regular can save you hundreds of milligrams a day.
