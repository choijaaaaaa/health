Stress incontinence — have you ever leaked a little urine when you cough, sneeze, or laugh? It's common after childbirth and in women past midlife, but most people never talk about it.

When the pelvic floor and urethral sphincter weaken, they can't hold back the sudden pressure from a cough or a laugh, and urine leaks out. Three everyday habits make that pressure worse: carrying extra weight, drinking a lot of caffeine, and skipping pelvic floor exercise.

First is carrying extra weight. Every extra pound adds pressure on the bladder, so even a small cough is enough to cause a leak.

Second is drinking a lot of coffee or other caffeinated drinks. Caffeine increases urine output and irritates the bladder, adding to the load your pelvic floor has to hold back.

And last is skipping pelvic floor exercise. Like any other muscle, it weakens without regular use, so its ability to hold back pressure keeps declining.

Losing just 8 percent of body weight cuts weekly leaks nearly in half, so start there and swap caffeinated drinks for water. Add Kegels — a 3 to 5 second squeeze and release, 10 reps, three times a day — and most women notice a real difference within 1 to 3 months.
