Nocturia - more than half of adults over 55 deal with this. Here are 3 causes beyond an enlarged prostate.

Waking up to urinate two or more times a night isn't just a normal part of aging - it's called nocturia, and more than half of adults over 55 deal with it. An enlarged prostate is only one possible cause - evening drinks, medication timing, and how well you sleep all play a role too.

Three things tend to drive this the most. Alcohol and caffeine in the evening, taking blood pressure or diuretic medication close to bedtime, and untreated sleep apnea.

First, alcohol and caffeine in the evening. Both act as diuretics and blunt the hormone that normally concentrates your urine overnight, so more fluid ends up leaving your body while you sleep.

Second, taking blood pressure or diuretic medication close to bedtime. It's still working while you sleep, which pushes urine output up right when you don't want it to. Asking your doctor about an earlier dose can make a real difference.

Third, loud snoring or sleep apnea. Each pause in breathing changes the pressure in your chest, which triggers a hormone that ramps up urine production overnight - treating the sleep apnea often cuts down the nighttime bathroom trips too.

Cutting alcohol and caffeine two to three hours before bed helps. Ask your doctor about taking blood pressure or diuretic medication earlier in the day, and if you snore loudly, ask about a sleep study.
