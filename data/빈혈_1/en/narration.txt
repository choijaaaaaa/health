If your legs won't stay still at night, it might not be restlessness — it could be low iron.

Restless legs syndrome affects nearly 1 in 10 American adults, and low iron is a common trigger behind it.

A few everyday habits might be quietly blocking the iron you're already eating.

Acid reducers like omeprazole lower stomach acid, which is what turns iron into a form you can absorb — one study found peak absorption cut nearly in half after a week of use.

Zinc supplements taken with iron compete for the same absorption pathway, cutting absorption by 30 to over 50 percent.

Red wine with dinner works the same way — its polyphenols bind to iron in your food, cutting absorption by about 28 percent.

Ask your doctor before changing any medication, and try pairing meals with vitamin C. Space zinc supplements at least two hours from iron, and wait about an hour after eating before having that glass of wine.
