Doubled over with cramps the moment your period starts, and reaching for painkillers before you've even gotten out of bed? What you eat those few days might be turning up the pain.

During your period, the uterine lining releases prostaglandins that make the uterus contract hard, and some everyday foods push that release into overdrive.

Coffee and energy drinks narrow blood vessels and can intensify uterine contractions - a review pooling three studies of over 1,000 women found a clear link between caffeine intake and worse period pain.

Wine and other alcohol dehydrate the body and can throw off pain signals, so cramps often feel sharper the next day.

Fried, salty snacks and fast food raise sodium, which pulls water into your tissues and adds to bloating, while the saturated fat in them fuels more prostaglandin production and stretches out the pain.

A few days before your period, swap coffee for decaf or herbal tea, trade wine for sparkling water with lime, and swap fries for baked options. Getting more omega-3s from salmon or walnuts may also help calm the inflammation behind the pain.
