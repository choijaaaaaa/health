Ever notice hiccups always hit at the worst moment? They happen when your diaphragm suddenly spasms and your vocal cords snap shut right after - a few everyday habits set that off more than you'd think.

Eating too fast is a big one. Wolfing down a meal makes you swallow air with it, and that trapped air stretches your stomach right against the diaphragm.

A sudden temperature swing can do it too - hot soup followed by an ice-cold drink. The quick shift irritates the nerves near your diaphragm.

Stress and excitement matter as well. A jolt of nervousness sends extra signals through the vagus nerve, which connects straight down to the diaphragm.

Just slow down and take smaller bites, and let hot and cold foods settle a few minutes apart. If hiccups start, hold your breath for 10 to 20 seconds and exhale slowly, or take a few slow sips of cold water.
