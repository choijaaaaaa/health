Stuck in traffic and your stomach suddenly cramps up, or a stressful workday sends you running to the bathroom — sound familiar? That's often IBS at work, a condition that affects an estimated 25 to 45 million Americans and is rooted in the gut-brain axis, the two-way signaling system between your brain and your gut. Three everyday habits throw that signaling off.

First, chronic stress. Ongoing stress sends abnormal signals from the brain down to the gut, speeding up or slowing down gut motility and making the gut far more sensitive to pain than it should be.

Second, fatty and fried foods. High-fat meals slow stomach emptying and trigger stronger, less coordinated gut contractions, which shows up as cramping, bloating, and an urgent need for the bathroom soon after eating.

Third, alcohol. Alcohol speeds up gut motility and irritates the intestinal lining directly, and that combination is a common trigger for diarrhea and cramping, especially the morning after drinking.

Calming the gut-brain axis starts small — just five minutes of slow, deep breathing can ease the signal. Swap fried and greasy meals for grilled or baked options, and space out alcoholic drinks with water in between. If symptoms last for months or come with unexplained weight loss, see a doctor to rule out other conditions.
