Gastroptosis — wondered if it was just you feeling stuffed after only a few bites? If you have a naturally thin frame, here's why. A leaner body means less fat and muscle cushioning around your stomach, so the ligaments holding it in place get less support, and the stomach sags lower than normal. Three everyday habits make that sag worse.

First, eating large meals too fast. When food rushes into a stomach that's already sitting lower than it should, the stomach wall stretches suddenly and digestion can't keep pace, so bloating and pressure hit hard.

Second, slouching or lying down right after eating. Slumped posture and lying flat both add extra pressure on your abdomen, pushing the sagging stomach down even further and making that heavy, pressured feeling linger.

Third, weak core muscles. Your abdominal wall is part of what holds your stomach in place, and when it's weak, the stomach has less support and keeps drifting lower over time.

So eat smaller portions spread across four or five meals instead of two or three big ones, and chew slowly. Stay upright for at least 30 minutes after eating instead of slouching or lying down, and if you do lie down, rest on your right side — that helps food move toward your pylorus with gravity instead of sitting heavy in a sagging stomach. Work in exercises that build real abdominal strength, like planks or gentle sit-ups, so your stomach gets more support over time — a gentle seated spinal twist is worth adding too, as an easy way to stay loose without putting extra pressure on your stomach.
