H. pylori infection is far more common than most people realize — about one in three adults in the US carries this bacteria, and most never know it until an ulcer or chronic stomach pain sends them to a doctor.

H. pylori burrows into the stomach lining and can trigger chronic gastritis, ulcers, and in some cases stomach cancer over time. Infection rates run highest among older adults, minority populations, and first-generation immigrants from regions where the bacteria is more common.

Here's the part that's changed recently: standard antibiotic treatment is getting harder to rely on. Resistance to clarithromycin, metronidazole, and levofloxacin — the antibiotics doctors used to reach for first — has climbed above 30% in the US. That resistance is why gastroenterologists updated their guidelines and now start with bismuth quadruple therapy instead.

Bismuth quadruple therapy combines a stomach-acid reducer, bismuth, and two antibiotics, taken together for fourteen days. It works around the resistant strains that single- or double-antibiotic regimens increasingly miss.

If you've had a stomach ulcer, unexplained stomach pain that won't quit, or a family history of stomach cancer, that's worth bringing up with your doctor — a simple breath, stool, or blood test can confirm whether H. pylori is behind it.

Getting tested and treated isn't just about clearing up discomfort now. It's the one step that actually lowers your long-term risk of ulcers and stomach cancer down the road.
