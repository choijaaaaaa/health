Acute indigestion after a big or fast meal happens for a clear reason. When you eat quickly or overeat, your stomach's ability to relax and stretch to hold that food gets overwhelmed — researchers call this impaired gastric accommodation, and it shows up in up to half of people with recurring stomach discomfort. On top of that, up to a third of people also have food sitting in the stomach longer than normal, which is what causes that heavy, bloated feeling after a meal.

Eating fast makes both problems worse. Your brain needs time to register that you're full, so when a meal is over in ten minutes instead of twenty, you end up eating past the point your stomach can comfortably handle.

Once it hits, a short walk is one of the best things you can do — it helps move food through your stomach and eases bloating, and even ten minutes makes a difference. What you do afterward matters too: staying upright for at least three hours before lying down keeps stomach acid from creeping back up your esophagus, so skip the couch right after a big meal.

Ginger tea and peppermint tea are two of the most commonly recommended home remedies — peppermint relaxes the muscles in your stomach, though it can make heartburn worse for some people, so drop it if that happens to you. An over-the-counter antacid and staying hydrated both help too, since your digestive system needs extra water to handle a big meal's workload.

If indigestion keeps coming back for weeks, or comes with weight loss, vomiting, or black stools, that's not just a big-meal problem anymore — see a doctor to rule out something else going on.
