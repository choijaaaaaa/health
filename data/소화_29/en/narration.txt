Matcha, if your stomach burns every time you drink it, there's a reason.

Matcha isn't steeped and strained like regular tea. You drink the whole leaf, ground into powder, so far more catechins and caffeine end up in your cup than in a cup of brewed green tea. Three things explain why matcha can leave your stomach burning: tannins, caffeine, and how people are drinking it lately.

First, tannins. Matcha's tannins stimulate acid production in the stomach lining directly, and a full dose of whole-leaf powder delivers a much stronger hit than steeped tea ever could.

Second, caffeine. Matcha has a mild, gentle reputation, but a well-made cup carries about as much caffeine as coffee. On an empty stomach, that caffeine pushes acid production even higher.

Third, the trend itself. Many people now treat a matcha latte as their entire breakfast, skipping food and drinking it straight on an empty stomach. That habit lets both the tannins and the caffeine hit unbuffered, right when your stomach is most sensitive.

Drink matcha after a meal, not instead of one, and keep it to two cups a day. Choose a lighter brew instead of a concentrated shot, and the burning stops.
