If your eyelids get heavy twenty minutes after lunch and won't lift till mid-afternoon, look at what's on your desk — the classic American lunch might be the problem.

High-glycemic foods spike blood sugar fast, so insulin floods in and can overshoot, crashing sugar below where it started — that crash cuts glucose to your brain and triggers the fatigue. Fatty foods work differently, linked to a hormone called cholecystokinin that acts on the vagus nerve and brings on sleepiness.

First, the sandwich on white bread. Refined white bread sits around a glycemic index of 75, digesting fast and sending blood sugar up, then crashing hard.

Second, the bag of chips. Fried and high in fat, linked to that cholecystokinin drowsiness — pairing it with a sugary drink seems to make the dip worse.

Third, the can of soda. One twelve-ounce can packs about 39 grams of sugar, near ten teaspoons, hitting your bloodstream fast and dropping just as fast.

Swap white bread for whole grain with some turkey, trade chips for a handful of nuts, and pour unsweetened iced tea instead of soda. One swap at a time makes the afternoon crash easier to handle.
