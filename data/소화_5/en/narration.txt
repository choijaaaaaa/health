If your stomach feels tight and bloated after eating, even on a day you ate clean, the real culprit might be one of these 3 everyday habits, not your last meal.

Sugar alcohols like sorbitol, maltitol, and xylitol in protein bars and shakes barely get absorbed, so gut bacteria ferment them in your colon and produce gas. Look for bars sweetened with monk fruit or stevia instead, and check the label for sugar alcohol grams.

Raw broccoli, kale, cabbage, and Brussels sprouts are loaded with raffinose, a sugar your gut can't break down until bacteria ferment it in the colon. Steaming them instead of eating them raw makes them much easier for your body to break down, so you end up with far less gas than eating them raw.

Sipping iced coffee or a smoothie through a straw pulls extra air into your stomach with every sip, a habit doctors call aerophagia. Drinking straight from the cup instead keeps that extra air out.
