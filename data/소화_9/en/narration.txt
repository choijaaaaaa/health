About 1 in 3 adults in industrialized countries lives with gastritis — are you one of them? Gastritis means the stomach lining is inflamed, and its normal barrier against acid breaks down, so acid ends up irritating the exposed tissue underneath. Three everyday habits make that worse.

First, drinking alcohol. Alcohol interferes with normal acid secretion and irritates the stomach lining directly, so acid attacks the very tissue meant to hold it back.

Second, chronic stress. Ongoing stress reduces blood flow to the stomach lining while also increasing acid production — a combination that leaves the lining more exposed and slower to repair itself.

Third, smoking. Nicotine travels through the bloodstream to the stomach, where it reduces protective mucus and blood flow to the lining while increasing acid production — weakening the lining's defenses from the inside.

Cut back on alcohol and give your stomach real recovery time between drinks. Build small stress breaks into your day — even a few minutes of slow breathing can ease the acid response. And if you smoke, quitting is one of the most direct ways to protect your stomach lining. If pain keeps coming back, see a doctor to check for H. pylori infection too.
