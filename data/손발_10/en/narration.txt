About 1 in 20 Americans deal with hyperhidrosis, and hands and feet are two of the most common spots — if yours are damp no matter the season, a few daily habits are usually keeping it going. Sweat glands in your palms and soles respond more to stress and nerve signals than to actual heat, so an overactive sympathetic nervous system can trigger dripping even in a cold room.

The sock and shoe combo is one of the biggest factors. Cotton absorbs moisture but doesn't pull it away from your skin, so once it's damp it stays damp — and sealed, rubber-soled shoes trap that moisture with nowhere to go.

Wearing the same pair of shoes two days in a row makes it worse. Shoes need 24 to 48 hours to fully dry out between wears, and skipping that means you're stepping into damp material before your feet even start sweating.

Caffeine plays a role too. It stimulates the nervous system that controls sweat glands, so heavy coffee or energy drink intake can trigger more sweating in your hands and feet specifically.

Switch to moisture-wicking socks made from merino wool or synthetic blends instead of cotton, and rotate between at least two pairs of shoes so each one gets a full day to dry. An aluminum chloride antiperspirant applied to clean, dry feet at night can cut sweating noticeably, and cutting back on caffeine helps too. If sweating is heavy enough to interfere with daily life, a dermatologist can walk you through iontophoresis or other clinical options.
