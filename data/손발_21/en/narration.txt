Onycholysis — if your nail keeps lifting away from the nail bed and turning white, there's a reason. Onycholysis is when the nail plate separates from the skin underneath, trapping air that turns the area white or yellow. Three habits usually cause it: aggressive filing, over-soaking in acetone, and back-to-back gel sessions.

First, aggressive buffing before a gel manicure. Technicians press hard to rough up the surface so gel sticks, and that pressure alone can start the lift. Ask for gentle buffing, or skip heavy sanding.

Second, soaking nails in acetone too long to strip old gel. Long soaks dissolve the oils holding the nail together, and picking at the edges peels off healthy layers with the polish. Have a technician file the gel down thin instead.

Third, booking gel sessions back to back with no break. Once the plate lifts, it doesn't reattach on its own — it has to grow out, which takes months. Leave nails bare for at least two weeks between gel sets.

If the lifting spreads or the nail changes color, get it checked by a dermatologist.
