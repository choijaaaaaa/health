Heel pain usually gets blamed on being on your feet all day - but the setup for it actually starts while you sleep. Your plantar fascia, the tissue supporting your arch, sits in a shortened position all night, and three habits pile more strain on top of that.

Ramping up your mileage too fast doesn't give the fascia time to adapt, so overload leads to repeated microtears. Standing for hours in flat, cushionless shoes on hard floors means every step's impact lands straight on that tissue instead of being absorbed. And taking that very first step out of bed without stretching first yanks the overnight-shortened fascia straight open, all at once.

Build up activity gradually, week by week. Pick shoes with real arch support for long standing days. And before you even swing your legs out of bed, circle your ankles and stretch your calves first - loosen that tissue before it takes your full weight, and the first-step pain eases off fast.
