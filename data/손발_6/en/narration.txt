Bunions - if the base of your big toe sticks out while the toe itself angles toward your other toes, here's why. Nearly one in four adults deals with this, and three habits speed it up.

First, wearing high heels or narrow, pointed shoes often. Heel height shifts your weight onto the ball of your foot, and a tight toe box crowds your big toe inward - that's what makes the joint stick out more.

Second, skipping toe and foot strengthening. The muscles holding your forefoot's arch weaken over time, so that crowding happens even faster.

Third, brushing off that early ache or bump instead of changing anything. The tendons and ligaments around the joint tighten into that position the longer it's ignored, and once they adapt, footwear changes alone can't pull it back.

Pick shoes with a wide toe box and a lower heel, and save heels for occasions. Toe-spreading stretches and towel-scrunch exercises daily keep that arch strong, and a toe spacer eases the pressure. See a podiatrist if it keeps getting worse.
