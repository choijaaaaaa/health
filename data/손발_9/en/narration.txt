Ingrown toenails send about 1 in 5 podiatry patients through the door, and they show up most in kids, young adults, and people in their 50s — not just seniors. If the skin next to your nail is turning red and puffy, a few everyday habits are usually behind it.

Tight, narrow-toed shoes are one of the two leading causes doctors point to. Constant pressure from the sides pushes the nail's growth path inward instead of straight out.

The other leading cause is how you're trimming. Rounding the corners or cutting too short — something most people were simply never taught not to do — lets the skin puff up over the nail bed, so the nail grows straight into it.

And once redness turns to spreading warmth or pus shows up, that's not a sign to wait it out — it means the skin is already infected, and if you have diabetes or poor circulation, that can escalate fast.

Switch to a wide toe box, and trim straight across, leaving the corners visible past the skin. If it's already swollen, soak your foot in warm water for 15 to 20 minutes and gently tuck a wisp of clean cotton under the corner. See a doctor if the redness spreads, pus shows up, or it hasn't improved in a week or two.
