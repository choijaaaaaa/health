Turns out, the pill millions of Americans take for blood sugar could be behind that tingling in your hands and feet — along with your soda habit and your desk chair.

Metformin can lower your B12 over time, and low B12 damages the nerves in your hands and feet. Ask your doctor for a B12 check, and add salmon or eggs to your plate.

Regular soda spikes your blood sugar again and again, and many people with prediabetes already have some nerve damage before they're ever diagnosed. Swap one soda for sparkling water, and walk after meals.

Sitting for hours with crossed legs compresses the nerves in your legs and cuts circulation to your feet. Stand and stretch every hour, and uncross your legs.

If the tingling sticks around, it's worth getting checked by a doctor — but these three changes are a solid place to start.
