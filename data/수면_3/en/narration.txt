Think sleep apnea only affects people who are overweight? Think again — research shows nearly a quarter of adults with sleep apnea have a normal body weight. It happens when your airway narrows or collapses during sleep, and three everyday habits make that narrowing worse.

First, sleeping flat on your back. Gravity pulls your tongue backward against your airway, and back sleepers get more than double the blockages side sleepers do.

Second, a pillow that's too high. Hours with your chin tucked toward your chest compresses and narrows your airway, making airflow harder.

Finally, sedatives or muscle relaxants. They relax the muscles around your airway too, and dull the reflex that's supposed to wake you when your breathing stops.

Sleep on your side, pick a lower pillow, and check with your doctor before relying on sleep medication. If you're still gasping awake or exhausted despite these changes, see a sleep clinic — this needs a real diagnosis, not just habit fixes.
