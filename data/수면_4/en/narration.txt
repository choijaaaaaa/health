Delayed sleep phase syndrome isn't about being lazy — it's your body clock running late. If you can't fall asleep until well past midnight no matter what you try, this might be why. Three habits keep pushing that clock later.

First, bright screens late at night suppress melatonin right when your brain should be winding down, delaying your clock even further.

Second, sleeping in on weekends. Staying up late, then catching up with a late wake time, creates what researchers call social jetlag — and since your clock resets around wake time, that inconsistency drags your rhythm later and later.

Third, not enough morning light. Morning light tells your clock a new day has started. Skip it, and your rhythm never resets.

Dim the lights an hour before bed, wake up at the same time daily, even on weekends, and get outside within thirty minutes of waking. Those three habits can help realign your clock. If your bedtime keeps drifting no matter what, a sleep specialist can help.
