If you've ever woken up unable to move and thought something was wrong with you, you're not alone - and you're not imagining it. English has no everyday word for this experience, so many people go through it without ever hearing it has a name: sleep paralysis, a well-documented part of how REM sleep works, not a sign anything's broken.

Doctors point to a few things that make it more likely. An irregular sleep schedule throws off your REM cycle. Sleeping flat on your back is linked to over four times as many episodes as side sleeping, since it raises airway resistance and disrupts that cycle further. It also shows up more in people dealing with anxiety or other mental health conditions - bedtime tension keeps your nervous system too alert to power down.

None of that means something's wrong with you. A steadier schedule, side sleeping, and managing stress before bed make episodes less frequent. One thing worth flagging to a doctor: if it keeps happening often, since repeated sleep paralysis can sometimes link to narcolepsy.
