If you've ever landed after a long flight or road trip with one leg swollen and achy, that could be deep vein thrombosis — a blood clot forming in a vein deep in your leg. The CDC estimates it affects up to nine hundred thousand Americans every year, and up to one hundred thousand people die from it, making it the third leading cause of cardiovascular death after heart attack and stroke. Here's why it happens: your calf muscles squeeze with every step to pump blood from your legs back up to your heart. Sit still for too long, especially in a cramped seat, and that pump stops working — blood pools in your lower legs and can start to clot. Moving regularly, staying hydrated, and wearing compression socks are the three habits that make the biggest difference.

Watch for the warning signs first. Swelling and pain in just one leg, along with skin that's red or warm to the touch, are the classic signs of a clot. If you suddenly feel short of breath or get chest pain, that clot may have traveled to your lungs — get to an emergency room right away instead of waiting it out. Smoking, pregnancy, birth control pills, and recent surgery all raise your risk further, so keep that in mind before a long trip.

First, move regularly. On long flights, road trips, or even long days at a desk, get up and walk around at least once an hour. If you can't get up, flex your ankles up and down while seated — that alone keeps your calf muscles working as a pump.

Second, stay hydrated. Dehydration makes your blood thicker and more likely to clot. Coffee and alcohol both make you lose fluids faster, so skip them on long trips and reach for water instead.

Third, wear compression socks. Knee-high compression socks apply gentle pressure that pushes blood flow back up your legs, and they're especially useful on long flights or in jobs where you're standing all day.

Move often, stay hydrated, and wear compression socks, and your risk of a blood clot drops significantly. But sudden swelling in just one leg, or shortness of breath, needs a doctor's visit right away — don't try to wait that out at home.
