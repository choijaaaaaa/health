Ever felt your heart suddenly pound or skip a beat when nothing startled you and you're just sitting still? If tests come back normal, that's usually not an arrhythmia - it's a symptom called palpitations.

Palpitations simply mean you're noticing your heartbeat more than usual - racing, pounding, or skipping. Arrhythmia, on the other hand, is an actual electrical problem confirmed on an EKG. Feeling palpitations doesn't mean you have an arrhythmia. Three everyday habits drive most cases: caffeine, stress, and lack of sleep.

Caffeine comes first. Coffee and energy drinks stimulate your central nervous system to keep you alert, and if you're sensitive to it, even a small amount can trigger a racing, jittery feeling. Cut back to one or two cups a day, and switch to decaf or herbal tea in the afternoon.

Stress is second. Tension and anxiety activate your sympathetic nervous system and release adrenaline, making your heart beat faster and harder. Chronic stress keeps that system switched on, so you can feel palpitations even during an ordinary day with nothing obviously wrong. Try a few minutes of slow breathing - inhale gently, then exhale for twice as long - which activates your parasympathetic nervous system and calms your heart rate. Cleveland Clinic also recommends vagal maneuvers, like splashing cold water on your face, to slow a racing heart.

Sleep deprivation is third. Not getting enough sleep disrupts your autonomic nervous system and keeps your sympathetic system overactive, so your heartbeat can feel irregular even at rest, and palpitations often get worse right before bed. Keep a consistent sleep and wake schedule, and cut caffeine and screens two to three hours before bedtime to let your nervous system wind down.

Cutting caffeine, calming stress through breathing, and sleeping on a consistent schedule noticeably reduce palpitations for most people. Low magnesium is also linked to palpitations, since magnesium helps regulate your heart rhythm - nuts and leafy greens are simple ways to close that gap. That said, if palpitations come with chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness, or fainting, or a racing heartbeat lasts more than 30 minutes even at rest, see a doctor - it could point to an underlying arrhythmia.
