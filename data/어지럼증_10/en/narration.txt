Someone near you suddenly looks off-balance and dizzy - do you know what to check before assuming it'll pass? BE FAST is the stroke warning system doctors use, and it's built for bystanders, because the person having a stroke often can't tell something's wrong themselves. About one in five strokes affects the back of the brain, and dizziness is often the very first sign anyone notices.

First, watch how they're moving. Can they walk a straight line, or do they suddenly lean or drift to one side? Trouble with balance and coordination, combined with dizziness, is the "B" in BE FAST, and it's one of the clearest signs something's wrong in the brain rather than just the inner ear.

Second, check their face, arm, and speech, the classic FAST signs. Ask them to smile: does one side droop? Ask them to raise both arms: does one drift down? Ask them to repeat a sentence: does it come out slurred? Even one uneven answer, alongside the dizziness, means it's time to call 911.

Don't wait to see if it passes, and don't drive them to the hospital yourself. Emergency stroke treatment works best within three to four and a half hours of symptoms starting, so calling 911 gets treatment started sooner than driving ever could.

If the person has high blood pressure, diabetes, or a history of smoking, take any unusual dizziness even more seriously, since their risk is already higher. Most dizziness is harmless, but balance loss, facial drooping, arm weakness, or slurred speech alongside it means it's a stroke emergency, not something to wait out.
