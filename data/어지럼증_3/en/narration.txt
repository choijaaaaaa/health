Roughly 1 in 40 people will deal with BPPV at some point - it's the single most common cause of vertigo, and a few everyday habits can raise your odds of getting it or having it come back.

BPPV happens when tiny calcium crystals from your inner ear drift into the canals that control balance, triggering that brief spinning feeling when you move your head a certain way.

Low vitamin D is one of the most studied risk factors - it's tied to weaker bone density, and since those crystals are built from calcium, poor calcium metabolism can make them more likely to break loose.

Long stretches of sitting or lying down without moving - a desk job, a long flight, days resting while sick - are linked to higher BPPV risk, since staying still may let loose crystals settle deeper into the canals.

Poor sleep quality itself - trouble falling or staying asleep - shows up in research as another factor tied to both getting BPPV and having it come back.

Try getting some sunlight or vitamin D-rich foods, taking short movement breaks through the day, and keeping a consistent sleep schedule - talk to a doctor if insomnia sticks around for weeks. Recurring spinning is worth getting checked out too.
