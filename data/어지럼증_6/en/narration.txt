If you keep getting dizzy spells with no headache at all, there's a reason. Vestibular migraine can cause vertigo and nausea without any head pain in roughly half of episodes, and research points to three triggers behind the pattern: sudden weather shifts, skipped meals, and loud, crowded spaces.

First, sudden weather and barometric pressure changes. Studies show that thirty to fifty percent of people with migraine report weather as a trigger, and rapid pressure shifts are the most commonly cited factor of all.

Second, skipping meals and not drinking enough water. Going too long without eating can drop your blood sugar, and mild dehydration alone can be enough to bring on an episode.

Third, loud noise and busy, visually crowded spaces. Grocery stores and shopping malls are common trigger settings because they combine sound, motion, and clutter all at once.

Watching the forecast on high-pressure-change days, keeping regular mealtimes with water nearby, and wearing noise-cancelling headphones in loud spaces cuts down how often these episodes strike.
