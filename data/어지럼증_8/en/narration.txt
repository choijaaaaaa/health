If your inner-ear tests and blood work come back normal but you're still dealing with dizziness, the cause might not be your ear at all — it could be your neck. Cervicogenic dizziness shows up in roughly eight percent of people with neck pain, and over half of patients referred for dizziness and balance issues also report neck and shoulder pain. Your neck is packed with sensory receptors that tell your brain where your head is, and the upper neck joints alone supply nearly half of that signal. When neck muscles tense up or alignment shifts, that signal gets distorted and clashes with what your eyes and ears are reporting, and that mismatch is what triggers the dizziness. Unlike the sudden spinning of BPPV, this type builds slowly with neck movement or long periods in one position. Three everyday habits are usually behind it.

First, sitting too long without a break. Office workers spend seventy to eighty percent of their working hours seated, and staying still that long keeps neck muscles in constant low-grade tension, distorting the position signals they send. Stand up and gently stretch your neck every thirty to sixty minutes, moving slowly instead of twisting suddenly.

Second, forward head posture. Up to eighty-five percent of office workers show some degree of it, and every inch your head drifts forward adds about ten pounds of extra force on your cervical spine. At sixty degrees of flexion, that load reaches sixty pounds. Bring your screen up to eye level and pull your chin in instead of tilting your head down.

Third, your sleep setup. A pillow that's too high or too flat flattens your neck's natural curve, so muscles stay tense all night and mornings can feel worse. Choose a pillow height that supports your neck's curve, and avoid sleeping on your stomach.

Fixing your posture and easing up on your neck can make a real difference over time. But if dizziness ever comes with double vision, slurred speech, or a sudden loss of balance, see a doctor right away — that combination can point to something more serious than a stiff neck.
