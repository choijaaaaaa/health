Ménière's disease, diagnosed in about 615,000 U.S. adults with up to 100,000 new cases every year — if your vertigo comes bundled with ringing ears, hearing changes, or a plugged-up feeling, it's not just ordinary dizziness.

Ménière's happens when fluid builds up in the inner ear, a condition called endolymphatic hydrops. Unlike BPPV or vestibular neuritis, attacks bring vertigo, tinnitus, fluctuating hearing loss, and aural fullness together, and can last anywhere from twenty minutes to several hours. Three everyday habits make attacks worse: sodium, caffeine and alcohol, and stress or poor sleep.

First, sodium. Extra salt makes your body retain fluid, which raises pressure in the already fluid-overloaded inner ear. Keep sodium under 1,500 to 2,000 milligrams a day, and swap canned and processed foods for fresh ingredients you season yourself.

Second, caffeine and alcohol. Caffeine constricts blood vessels and reduces blood flow to the inner ear, while alcohol diffuses directly into the inner ear fluid and disrupts its balance — both can trigger an episode. Swap coffee for decaf or herbal tea, and reach for sparkling water instead of a second drink.

Third, stress and inconsistent sleep. Both disrupt the nervous system signals that regulate blood flow and fluid balance in the inner ear. Keep a consistent sleep schedule, and swap your phone before bed for a few minutes of light stretching.

If vertigo keeps showing up with ringing ears or hearing changes, don't just wait it out — see an ENT for a proper diagnosis. Cutting back on sodium, caffeine, and alcohol, plus steady sleep, meaningfully cuts down how often attacks happen.
