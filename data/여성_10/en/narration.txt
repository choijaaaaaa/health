Endometriosis, the pelvic pain that doesn't stop when your period does, isn't just something you have to live with — the fats in your diet can shift your risk of it.

About one in ten women of reproductive age has endometriosis, tissue that acts like the uterine lining but grows outside it, on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, or pelvic lining. Every month, that misplaced tissue still responds to estrogen, which triggers bleeding, inflammation, and scar tissue where it doesn't belong.

Unlike typical menstrual cramps, this pain often continues before and after your period, radiates to your lower back or thighs, and can show up during sex or bowel movements. Roughly forty percent of women with endometriosis experience infertility, and among women being treated for infertility, twenty-five to fifty percent turn out to have the condition.

A twelve-year Harvard study following over one hundred thousand nurses found two specific fats that move the needle. First, trans fat raises risk by forty-eight percent — the biggest sources were fried foods eaten outside the home, especially french fries, along with margarine and crackers. Second, long-chain omega-3 fatty acids cut risk. Women with the highest omega-3 intake were twenty-two percent less likely to be diagnosed than those with the lowest intake.

If your pain lingers well past your period and shows up during sex or bowel movements, don't just wait it out — talk to a gynecologist. Cutting back on fried food and adding more fatty fish like salmon can support your treatment plan too.
