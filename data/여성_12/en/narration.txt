Ever get a sharp twinge on one side of your lower belly, right in the middle of your cycle, and it's not even close to your period? That's ovulation pain, and it affects more than 40% of women of reproductive age — for many of them, it happens almost every single month.

Here's what's going on inside your body. Right before an egg is released, the growing follicle stretches the surface of the ovary. Then, when it ruptures, the fluid it releases irritates the lining of your abdomen. Prostaglandins — the same compounds involved in period cramps — drive both of those steps.

The pain is usually felt on just one side, matching whichever ovary released the egg that month, and it can last anywhere from a few minutes to about 48 hours.

There are two simple things that help most people. First, heat — a heating pad or hot water bottle on your lower belly relaxes the muscles and takes the edge off fast. Second, an NSAID like ibuprofen taken at the first sign of pain blocks the same prostaglandins that are causing it.

It also helps to track your cycle. Mark the days you expect ovulation on a calendar or period app, so you can keep pain relief on hand before the ache even starts.

That said, this isn't always just ovulation pain. See a doctor if the pain lasts more than 48 hours, or if it comes with nausea, vomiting, or dizziness. Sudden, severe pain on one side needs urgent care — it could be a sign of ovarian torsion, an ectopic pregnancy, or a ruptured cyst, and none of those should wait.
