Menopause joint pain: if your knees ache going down the stairs and your fingers feel stiff every morning, save this.

Estrogen protects cartilage, calms inflammation in the joints, and keeps ligaments flexible. Once it drops after menopause, more than half of women develop joint pain, and those who never used hormone therapy show nearly triple the risk of osteoarthritis. Three habits matter most here: the muscles around your joints, your weight, and omega-3s.

First, the muscles around your joints. Weaker muscles can't absorb shock from walking or stairs, so that impact lands directly on the joint. Start with light strength training for your thighs and calves, and build up gradually.

Second, your weight. Extra weight multiplies the load on your knees, which is why going down stairs often hurts most. Low-impact cardio like walking or swimming helps bring that weight down without adding more stress.

Finally, omega-3s. Skipping fatty fish means missing fats that calm joint inflammation, so pain tends to stick around longer. Add salmon or mackerel to your meals, or fill the gap with a supplement.

Keep up these three habits together, and the pain noticeably eases.
