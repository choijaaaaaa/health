Most people blame heavy periods and pelvic pressure on stress or genetics. Research points to something else instead. Fibroids are non-cancerous growths that respond to estrogen and progesterone. They're incredibly common. Up to 80% of women develop them by age 50, according to the NIH. A few everyday habits appear to raise that risk even further.

First, a diet heavy in red meat. One study found women who ate the most red meat had about 1.7 times higher odds of fibroids. Diets rich in green vegetables and fruit were linked to lower risk.

You don't have to cut red meat out completely. Swapping in fish or legumes a couple of times a week may help. Loading your plate with vegetables helps too.

Second, regular alcohol use. Alcohol is linked to higher estrogen levels in premenopausal women. Daily drinkers face notably higher fibroid risk than non-drinkers.

You don't need to quit drinking altogether. Building in a few alcohol-free days each week may help. Cutting back on how much you have in one sitting helps too.

Third, low vitamin D. Women with sufficient vitamin D had about 32% lower odds of fibroids than those with a deficiency, an NIH-funded study found.

Getting some sunlight can help close that gap. So can vitamin D-rich foods like salmon or eggs, or a supplement. A Mediterranean-style diet is also linked to lower fibroid risk overall.

Fibroids also tend to show up earlier and more severely in Black women. Don't brush off symptoms just because you're young. If your periods are unusually heavy, an ultrasound can confirm what's going on. Treatment options vary by size and location. Cutting back on red meat and alcohol while adding more vitamin D is a simple place to start.
