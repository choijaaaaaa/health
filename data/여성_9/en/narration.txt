Skip your period for months before age 40? How fast you get diagnosed can shape your bone health for decades.

This is called primary ovarian insufficiency, or POI - your ovaries stop working normally before 40 and estrogen drops. Causes include ovarian surgery and cancer treatment, and autoimmune conditions - though for many women, the exact cause is never identified.

Surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation can damage ovarian tissue directly. Separately, autoimmune conditions are estimated to cause anywhere from 4 to 30 percent of cases - the immune system mistakenly targets the ovaries, and follicle function drops off faster than expected.

Here's what most people miss: the timing of diagnosis changes bone outcomes. In one study, women diagnosed within 6 months of their first symptoms had normal bone density 45 percent of the time, with osteoporosis in just 7.5 percent of cases. Wait more than 3 years, and osteoporosis rates climb to 25 percent.

If your periods stop or turn irregular for 3 months or more before 40, get evaluated - a simple hormone blood test can confirm it. Hormone therapy is typically recommended up to the average age of menopause to help support bone and heart health. Pair it with enough calcium, vitamin D, and weight-bearing exercise like walking or strength training.
