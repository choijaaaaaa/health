It's not garlic or coffee - the real culprits behind your breath might be in your wine glass, your fridge, and your medicine cabinet.

Dry mouth means less saliva, and bacteria multiply fast, breaking proteins into the sulfur compounds behind bad breath. Alcohol, dairy, and allergy meds all speed that up.

Alcohol blocks the hormone that helps your body hold onto water, so your mouth dries out fast - alternate every drink with a glass of water to keep saliva flowing.

Milk and cheese proteins get broken down by bacteria into hydrogen sulfide, a rotten-egg-smelling compound of its own. Rinse with water or crunch on an apple after dairy to help clear it out.

Common antihistamines block the signal that tells your glands to make saliva, and over 400 medications list dry mouth as a side effect. Keep water close, chew sugar-free gum, and ask your pharmacist about a lower-drying option.
