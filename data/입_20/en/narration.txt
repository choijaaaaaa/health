If the corners of your mouth keep cracking and stinging, it's rarely just dry skin — it usually comes down to three things.

The first is saliva sitting in the corners of your mouth. Licking your lips or sleeping with your mouth open leaves saliva pooling there, and as it dries it pulls out more moisture than it adds. That damp-then-dry cycle creates the perfect environment for bacteria and yeast like Candida to take hold. Instead of licking, dab the area dry and seal it with a thin layer of petroleum jelly.

Second, a diet low in B vitamins. Riboflavin, or vitamin B2, plays a direct role in keeping skin and mucous membranes intact, and low B2 intake is linked to roughly a quarter of angular cheilitis cases. Eggs, dairy, and leafy greens are some of the easiest ways to close that gap.

Third, fatigue and a weakened immune system. Cold, dry weather combined with poor sleep thins the skin barrier, so even small irritation splits it open. Consistent rest gives your skin the chance to actually repair itself, not just patch over the crack.
