Enamel erosion works differently from a cavity, the American Dental Association notes - acid dissolves the mineral surface directly, and once it's gone, it doesn't grow back.

If you're brushing twice a day and still noticing new sensitivity or thinning enamel, it might not be about technique - certain everyday drinks could be quietly behind it.

Cola-based sodas are a leading culprit - even sugar-free versions stay acidic from carbonation and phosphoric acid, so every can adds a little more wear.

Citrus juices like orange juice sit in a similarly low pH range, and can be just as hard on enamel as soda, especially with regular sipping.

Sticky candy and gummies work differently - sugar feeds bacteria in your mouth, and the acid they produce lingers on tooth surfaces even longer than a drink would.

A simple fix: rinse with water after anything acidic or sugary, then wait about 30 minutes before brushing - and try a straw for juice.
