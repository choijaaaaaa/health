Most people think brushing alone keeps tartar away. It doesn't — plaque can start hardening into that rough, cement-like layer in as little as 24 hours, and three gaps in a normal routine let it happen.

Your toothbrush never reaches the tight spaces between your teeth, so plaque left there hardens undisturbed. Saliva flow drops by up to 90% overnight, so skipping the pre-bed brush leaves bacteria sitting for six to eight hours with nothing washing them away. And every sugar hit from grazing on snacks drops your mouth's pH within minutes, feeding the bacteria that build up plaque even faster.

None of that means brushing is pointless — it means brushing needs backup. Add floss or an interdental brush to reach what your toothbrush misses, never let the night brush slide no matter how tired you are, and cut back on how often you snack. Small gaps, closed early, keep tartar from ever getting a foothold.
