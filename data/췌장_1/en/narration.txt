Sharp pain in your upper right belly after a fatty meal, plus eyes turning a little yellow? That points to your gallbladder, not just your stomach.

In the US, gallstones cause 35 to 40 percent of acute pancreatitis cases - the top cause. Your gallbladder and pancreas share one drainage opening into the small intestine. When a stone blocks it, digestive enzymes back up into the pancreas and it starts digesting itself.

Crash dieting raises that risk. Losing weight too fast makes your liver dump extra cholesterol into bile faster than your gallbladder can handle, so it crystallizes into stones. A diet low in fiber, fruit, and vegetables is linked to more gallstones too.

If you are losing weight, aim for about half a kilogram to one kilogram a week, not a crash. Add beans, oats, and vegetables for fiber, and keep some healthy fat in meals - it helps your gallbladder empty regularly.

Pain that will not quit, fever, or yellowing skin and eyes means an ER visit, not wait and see.
