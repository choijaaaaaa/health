Taking allergy medicine every day but still waking up mouth-breathing because your nose won't clear? The culprit might be sitting on your plate, not in your medicine cabinet.

Many people with rhinitis notice sneezing and congestion flare up after certain meals - some foods raise histamine or inflammation in the body, and that hits your nose hard.

Milk and cheese contain casein, a protein some research links to a thicker, stickier feel in nasal mucus, which is why many people feel more stuffed up after having dairy.

Wine and other alcohol carry natural histamine and widen the blood vessels in your nose, so even one drink can trigger sudden congestion - separate from any real allergy.

Bacon, salami, and aged cheeses like cheddar are some of the highest-histamine foods around, and eating a lot of them can make sneezing worse.

Try oat or almond milk instead of dairy, pick a clear spirit like vodka soda over wine, and swap cured meats for fresh turkey or chicken breast - small swaps that can ease the reaction.
