Nose picking is a habit far more people share than you'd guess — most adults do it occasionally, and some do it several times a day without even noticing. It feels harmless, but it can open the door to infections that are easy to avoid once you know what's going on.

One risk is nasal vestibulitis, a bacterial infection near the opening of your nostril. Picking irritates and can break the skin just inside your nose, and that small injury gives bacteria an easy way in. The area often ends up red, swollen, and tender to the touch.

The bigger concern is Staphylococcus aureus. A large-scale study of the nasal microbiome from the Wellcome Sanger Institute, the University of Cambridge, and Imperial College London confirmed that roughly 30 percent of people carry S. aureus in their nose without any symptoms at all. It's harmless there — until it gets into a wound. If a scratch from picking lets it into deeper tissue, it can cause a painful infection that sometimes needs medical treatment.

So why does the urge keep coming back? Usually it's dryness. When the nasal lining dries out, mucus hardens and that trapped, crusty feeling makes you want to dig it out — which just irritates the lining further and restarts the cycle.

A saline spray is the easiest fix. Rinsing or misting with saline loosens dried mucus without any decongestant ingredients, so it's safe to use as often as you need. Keeping indoor humidity between 40 and 60 percent — especially in air-conditioned or heated rooms — also keeps the lining from drying out in the first place, and a thin layer of petroleum jelly just inside the nostrils can help overnight.

If allergies or a lingering cold are behind the congestion, treating that underlying cause cuts down the urge more than anything else. And if you do need to clear something out, wash your hands first and use a tissue instead of a bare finger — one hand alone can carry tens of thousands of bacteria, so that one step keeps a bit of dryness from turning into an infection. See a doctor if the area stays swollen, red, or painful for more than a couple of days.
