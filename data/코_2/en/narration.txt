About 60% of Americans get a nosebleed at some point in their life - but if yours keep coming back, a few daily habits might be to blame.

Nosebleeds happen when tiny vessels in the nasal lining tear. Allergy medication, frequent flying, and blowing your nose too hard are common triggers.

Antihistamines and decongestants dry out your nasal lining, so it cracks and bleeds more easily.

Airplane cabins run far drier than a normal room, and pressure changes on takeoff and landing add extra strain on those vessels.

Blowing hard during a cold or allergy flare puts pressure right where most nosebleeds start.

Try a saline spray on flights, blow gently one side at a time, and ask your doctor about allergy medication if it seems to be drying you out.
