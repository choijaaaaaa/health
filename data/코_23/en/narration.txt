If your nose turns unusually pale or you feel sharp, worsening pain after a filler injection, pay attention right away. This isn't ordinary swelling — it can be an early sign that a blood vessel has been blocked.

Nose vessels sit close to the artery that supplies your eye, so when filler blocks one of them, blood flow stops and the tissue starts losing oxygen. Here are three warning signs worth knowing.

First, pain that doesn't match the procedure. Normal bruising eases over time, but pain from a blocked vessel keeps getting worse.

Second, changing skin color. The area turns pale first, then within hours shifts to a mottled, purplish tone — a slow change easy to mistake for a bruise.

Third, and most critical, is the golden window. Tissue can only survive without oxygen for a few hours, so waiting even a day can make recovery far harder.

If the area stays pale and the pain won't ease, contact your provider or go to urgent care immediately. Before getting filler, ask whether the clinic uses ultrasound guidance and keeps hyaluronidase on hand for emergencies — that's the clearest way to stay safe.
