Nasal dryness, up to 65 percent of people using a CPAP machine report it, and it is not just from the airflow.

The lining inside your nose needs constant moisture to stay flexible, and when it dries out it cracks, crusts, and bleeds easily. CPAP therapy, frequent flying, and forced-air home heating all speed that up.

First is CPAP therapy without enough humidification. The constant pressurized airflow strips moisture from the nasal lining all night. Turn on your machine's heated humidifier and raise the setting if your nose still feels dry by morning.

Second is frequent air travel. Airplane cabin air holds as little as ten to twenty percent humidity, far drier than any room on the ground. Use a saline nasal spray before boarding and again during long flights to keep the lining moist.

Third is forced-air furnace heating. Winter indoor humidity in homes with forced-air heat can drop to fifteen to twenty-five percent, well below the thirty to fifty percent nasal tissue needs. Run a humidifier near your bed so the moisture actually reaches you.

Humidifying your CPAP, spraying saline before flights, and running a bedroom humidifier all season make a real difference for nasal dryness.
