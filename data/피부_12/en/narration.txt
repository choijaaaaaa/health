Your face flushes and burns all the time, and it's not just blushing - it might be rosacea. Sun exposure, emotional stress, and heat are three of the biggest triggers, and getting to know yours can make a real difference.

Rosacea causes persistent redness and visible blood vessels on the cheeks, nose, and forehead, and it's often mistaken for acne or simply blushing easily. In patient surveys, about a third report a flare-up every few days.

Sun exposure is the number one trigger - 81% of patients say it worsens their symptoms, because UV rays dilate and damage blood vessels under the skin.

Emotional stress is a close second, reported by 79% of patients. Stress hormones dilate blood vessels and trigger sudden flushing within minutes.

Hot weather, wind, and heavy exercise round out the top triggers, each raising skin temperature and expanding facial vessels.

Wear broad-spectrum SPF 30+ every morning and reapply outdoors, build in short stress breaks during the day, and exercise in cooler hours with a cold compress on hand. More than 90% of patients who identify and avoid their personal triggers see real improvement - a dermatologist can help confirm what's actually going on.
