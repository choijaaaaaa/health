Protein shake after the gym, a latte with milk, then a bag of chips at your desk - a pretty ordinary day, and one that shows up a lot in people dealing with jawline and cheek acne. Nearly half of American women in their 20s deal with it.

High-glycemic carbs and dairy proteins both raise insulin and IGF-1, a hormone that increases oil production and thickens pores - research points at whey protein, skim milk, and high-glycemic snacks like chips.

Whey protein shakes are the clearest case - dermatologists have reported breakouts in bodybuilders within weeks of starting whey.

Skim milk carries a stronger acne link than whole milk, likely because processing concentrates its oil-stimulating hormones.

Chips and other high-glycemic snacks add up too - one landmark trial found low-glycemic eaters improved nearly twice as much over 12 weeks.

Swap the whey shake for a couple of eggs, trade skim milk for unsweetened soy milk, and reach for almonds instead of chips.
