Tank top season means your underarms are out - and if they look darker than the rest of your skin, it's probably not your imagination. Dermatology clinics see this complaint constantly, and it usually comes down to three everyday habits.

First, shaving with a dull blade or against the grain. That tugging motion irritates the hair follicle, and skin responds to repeated irritation by producing extra melanin - the same defense reaction that darkens a healing scrape.

Second, fragrance and dyes in antiperspirants and deodorants, especially when applied right after shaving. Freshly shaved skin absorbs those ingredients more easily, so the irritation - and the pigment it leaves behind - builds up faster.

Third, tight workout gear. Sports bras and compression tanks that trap sweat and rub the same spot during every workout create the same friction-driven pigmentation over time.

Switch to a sharp single-blade razor and shave with the grain, pick a fragrance-free deodorant, and reach for looser, moisture-wicking fabric when you can. Underarms usually lighten up fast once the irritation stops.
