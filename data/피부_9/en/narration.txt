Still picking at breakouts even though you have melasma? Stop right there - that habit, plus applying sunscreen just once a day and scrubbing your skin too hard, is quietly making it worse.

Melasma shows up when UV exposure pushes melanocytes to overproduce pigment, and hormones make it worse - melasma affects an estimated 5 to 6 million women in the US, and more than 80% of people with melasma have a history of using oral contraceptives.

First, applying sunscreen once in the morning and calling it done. Sun protection fades over just a few hours, so melanocytes keep getting triggered for the rest of the day.

Second, picking at breakouts or scrubbing irritated skin. That friction and inflammation is why 66.8 percent of people with melasma develop dark marks after acne, compared to 24.1 percent of people without melasma.

Reapply SPF 30 or higher every 2 hours - a tinted mineral sunscreen with iron oxide blocks visible light too. Swap picking for a pimple patch and a gentle, fragrance-free cleanser instead of a scrub. Citrus, kiwi, almonds, colorful vegetables, and salmon give your skin the vitamin C and E it needs to repair UV damage.
