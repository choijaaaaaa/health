If you've scrolled TikTok lately, you've probably seen "secret menu" coffee orders at Starbucks or Dunkin' - a dozen pumps of syrup, extra drizzle, and two or three flavor shots stacked into one cup. Some of these videos get millions of views, but the sugar hiding in them deserves a closer look.

One widely covered order was reported to pack in more than 20 teaspoons of added sugar in a single iced coffee - more than what's in a large soda, just dressed up as coffee.

Three things make these drinks worse for blood sugar than people expect. First, sugar dissolved in liquid hits your bloodstream faster than sugar in solid food, since there's no fiber or fat slowing digestion down. Second, stacking multiple syrups and flavor pumps makes it easy to lose track of just how much sugar you're actually adding. Third, caffeine itself can blunt insulin sensitivity for a few hours, so the spike from all that sugar tends to run higher and last longer on top of it.

None of this means coffee is the problem. Ask for sugar-free syrup, cut the pumps in half, or swap extra syrup for a splash of milk - and skip ordering it on an empty stomach, since food alongside it slows down how fast the sugar hits.
