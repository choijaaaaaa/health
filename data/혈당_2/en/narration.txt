That mid-afternoon crash where you're suddenly starving and can't focus? For a lot of people it traces back to a blood sugar spike after breakfast or lunch, and one common baking spice has decades of federal research behind it as a way to blunt that spike.

USDA scientists at the Beltsville Human Nutrition Research Center found that under half a teaspoon of cinnamon a day for 40 days lowered blood sugar by about 20 percent in people with type 2 diabetes.

A 2024 trial in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition backed that up - four grams of cinnamon a day for four weeks measurably lowered blood glucose on continuous monitors in adults with prediabetes.

That's more than a light dusting - stir it into oatmeal, coffee, or a smoothie at each meal.

One catch: most cinnamon on US shelves is cassia, which contains coumarin and can stress your liver in large amounts over time. Ceylon cinnamon, labeled "true cinnamon," is the safer daily pick. Check with your doctor first if you're on diabetes medication.

Cinnamon alone won't fix blood sugar control - regular movement and whole foods still make the biggest difference.
