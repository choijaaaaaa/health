Low blood sugar after meals - the shaking and cold sweats have a real cause. Here are 3 triggers behind reactive hypoglycemia.

Feeling shaky, sweaty, or lightheaded two to four hours after eating is a classic sign of reactive hypoglycemia - a blood sugar crash that can happen even without diabetes. It starts when a meal spikes your blood sugar fast, so the pancreas releases too much insulin to bring it back down, and glucose ends up dropping below normal. Your brain runs short on fuel, and adrenaline kicks in, which is what causes the shakiness and cold sweats.

Three things tend to trigger it most. White rice, pasta, and bread, candy and sugary drinks, and for some people, a history of stomach surgery.

First, white rice, white pasta, and white bread digest fast and spike blood sugar quickly, which can trigger an oversized insulin response.

Second, candy and sugary drinks like soda or juice hit your bloodstream even faster than solid food does, making the crash sharper.

And for some people, past gastric bypass or other stomach surgery changes how quickly sugar reaches the bloodstream, making reactive hypoglycemia more likely even years later.

Pairing carbs with protein and healthy fat slows digestion and softens the spike. Eating smaller meals every three to four hours keeps blood sugar steadier throughout the day.
